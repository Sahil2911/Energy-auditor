# Assessment — Day 1

**Question:** 18th sitting, Set A, N-1 — natural gas vs furnace oil boiler,
CO₂ offset by green power. 20 marks.

**Score: 2 / 20 as marked. But that number is misleading — see below.**

## What happened

Correctly calculated the furnace oil requirement:

```
    FO = 8000 × (665 − 90) / (0.84 × 10000) = 547.62 kg/hr
```

This matches the model answer exactly and earns its full 2 marks. The direct
method was applied correctly, feedwater enthalpy was read straight from the
temperature, and the units were consistent.

Could not calculate CO₂ for either boiler.

## The marks were not available

This was a mis-set question, and the responsibility is the Tutor's.

Of the 20 marks, only **2** were reachable with Day 1 material:

| Step | Marks | Needed |
|---|---|---|
| Theoretical air | 3 | Day 2 |
| Mass of dry flue gas | 2 | Day 2 |
| L1 dry flue gas loss | 2 | Day 3 |
| L2 hydrogen loss | 2 | Day 3 |
| NG fuel rate | 2 | Depends on indirect-method efficiency |
| **FO fuel rate** | **2** | **Day 1 — achieved** |
| CO₂ both fuels | 2 | Carbon balance, untaught |
| Increase in CO₂ | 2.5 | Depends on the above |
| Green energy | 2.5 | Depends on the above |

The furnace oil branch works from Day 1 knowledge because its efficiency (84%)
is **given**. The natural gas branch requires efficiency to be *derived* by the
indirect method — three days of material away.

**She solved every part she had been equipped to solve.** As a diagnostic of
Day 1 teaching, this is a pass, not a failure.

## The one genuine gap

The CO₂ calculation. Not taught anywhere in Day 1, and it is a real Paper 4
topic — emission and offset questions appear in the sustainability-flavoured
problems that recent papers favour.

```
    CO₂ (kg/hr) = fuel rate × carbon fraction × 3.67
```

Notable: the factor 3.67 is 44/12, which already appears in the Route 1 dry flue
gas formula as `C × (44/12)`. The student had no way to make that connection
because neither had been taught. Day 2 now teaches them together and names the
link explicitly.

## Verdict

Day 1 teaching worked. The direct method and evaporation ratio landed, and were
applied correctly to an unfamiliar problem on the first attempt.

## Instruction to Tutor

Three changes, all applied:

1. **Swap Days 2 and 3.** Combustion stoichiometry must precede the indirect
   method — the latter cannot be computed without theoretical air, excess air
   and dry flue gas mass. The original ordering was wrong.
2. **Replace Day 1 Block C** with a self-contained evaporation-ratio question
   (18th L-1). Verified solvable with Day 1 material alone.
3. **Teach the carbon balance in Day 2, section A5**, explicitly linking 3.67 to
   the 44/12 in the dry flue gas formula.
