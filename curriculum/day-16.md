# ANSWERS AND MARKING

<details><summary>SECTION I</summary>

1. **False.** Dew point is the temperature at which air becomes saturated. Drier
   air has to be cooled *further* before it condenses, so a **lower** dew point
   means **less** moisture.
2. **True.** More CO₂ means less excess air diluting the flue gas — carbon burnt to
   completion in the minimum air. (Watch the limit: CO₂ falls again if combustion
   goes incomplete and CO forms.)
3. **True.** Outdoor air arrives hotter *and* more humid, so it loads the coil both
   ways. `concept-distinctions` entry 13 — infiltration is one of only three items
   with a latent part.
4. **Lower.** On balanced draft the ID fan handles the FD air **plus** the
   combustion products **plus** any air in-leakage, all at a higher temperature and
   therefore a larger volume. The FD fan handles less.
5. **False.** A heat pump delivers what it absorbs **plus** the work put in:
   `3440/860 + 1.5 = 4.0 + 1.5 = 5.5 kW`, not 6.6. Same conservation statement as
   the chiller rejection in Day 15 Rung 163, seen from the hot end.
6. **Higher.** A smaller terminal temperature difference means the steam condenses
   closer to the cooling water temperature, which takes more surface or better heat
   transfer. Lower TTD is a *better* condenser.
7. **False.** Nameplate rating is the **shaft output** the motor can deliver, not
   its electrical input. Input = output/efficiency, so a 100 kW motor at 92% draws
   ~109 kW at full load — and far less at part load.
8. **False.** They are **reciprocals**: `COP = 3.517/(kW/TR)`. Higher kW/TR is a
   worse machine. `reciprocal-traps` entry 7.
9. **True.** Only carbon, hydrogen and sulphur burn. Oxygen in the fuel actually
   *reduces* the air required — which is why `A_th` carries the `(H₂ − O₂/8)` term.
10. **False.** Head is a height and is **independent of density** — a centrifugal
    pump develops the same metres on brine as on water. **Pressure** is
    proportional to density, and so is the power `ρgQH`. `concept-distinctions`
    entry — head vs pressure, Day 8.

</details>

<details><summary>SECTION II</summary>

**L-1 — thermic fluid**

The heat load belongs to the **fluid**, not to either fuel. Compute it once.

```
   mass  = 60 × 826                                     = 49 560 kg/hr
   Q     = 49 560 × (2.223/4.186) × (230 − 210)         = 5,26,383 kCal/hr

   Oil:        5,26,383/(10 000 × 0.80) = 65.80 kg/h → × 70 = ₹4606/hr
   Briquettes: 5,26,383/(3200 × 0.65)   = 253.1 kg/h → ×  7 = ₹1772/hr
```

**Recommend briquettes** — about **one quarter** of the operating cost, despite the
worse heater efficiency, because the fuel is a tenth of the price per kCal.

> ⚠️ **BEE's model answer uses Cp = 2.233 and gets 5,28,750 kCal/hr → ₹4626 and
> ₹1779.** The data table prints **2.223**. Either way briquettes win by the same
> margin. Use the printed value and note the discrepancy in one line.
>
> **This is the Day 15 Rung 164 question in disguise.** The invariant is the heat
> load. Neither fuel's efficiency belongs anywhere in the other fuel's arithmetic.

**L-2 — EPI and AAHEPI**

```
   Total energy = 9,73,569 + 25,167                      = 9,98,736 kWh/yr
   Area         = 18 000 − 8 000                         = 10 000 m²

   EPI    = 9,98,736/10 000                              = 99.87 ≈ 100 kWh/yr/m²
   AAHEPI = 100/2500                                     = 0.04 kWh/h/m²
                                                         = 40 Wh/h/m²
```

**The whole question is the subtraction.** Parking, road and basement are not
conditioned, so they do not belong in the denominator of an energy performance
index. Divide by 18,000 and you get 55.5 — a plausible-looking wrong answer.

*(EPI = annual energy per m² of conditioned built-up area. AAHEPI = EPI per
operating hour — it lets you compare a building running 2500 h/yr against one
running 8000.)*

</details>

<details><summary>N-1 — biogas cogeneration</summary>

```
  i)   generator heat = 900 × 860/(0.97 × 0.98 × 0.85)   = 9,57,909 kCal/h
       Δh across turbine = 720 − 660                     = 60 kCal/kg
       steam to turbine  = 9,57,909/60                   = 15 965 kg/h = 15.96 TPH

  ii)  chiller heat = 2000 × 3024/0.8                    = 75,60,000 kCal/h
       steam to chiller = 75,60,000/(660 − 100)          = 13 500 kg/h = 13.5 TPH

  iii) steam to process = 15.96 − 13.5                   = 2.46 TPH

  iv)  Condensate returns from the chiller at 100 °C; the process returns NIL,
       so its share arrives as 30 °C makeup. Mix them:
       T = (13.5 × 100 + 2.46 × 30)/(13.5 + 2.46)        = 89.2 °C
       biogas = 15 960 × (720 − 89.2)/(0.75 × 7000)      = 1917.6 Sm³/hr

  v)   VAM rejects = cooling load + the steam heat that drove it
                   = 2000 × 3024 + 13 500 × (660 − 100)
                   = 60,48,000 + 75,60,000               = 1,36,08,000 kCal/h
       CT flow = 1,36,08,000/(3 × 1000)                  = 4536 m³/hr
       pump kW = (4536/3600) × 15 × 9.81/(0.75 × 0.93)   = 265.8 kW
```

**Two things this question is really testing.**

**Part (iv):** "NIL CONDENSATE" on the schematic is not decoration. The process
consumes its steam, so that fraction of the feedwater is 30 °C makeup, not 100 °C
return. Miss it and the biogas figure is 10% low.

**Part (v) is Rung 163 from the other side.** The chiller's rejection is the
cooling load **plus the energy that drove it** — and here the driving energy
(75.6 lakh kCal/h of steam) is *larger* than the cooling load (60.5 lakh). That is
what a COP of 0.8 means. Use only the 2000 × 3024 and the pump comes out 44% small.
</details>

<details><summary>N-2 — mall chiller plant</summary>

```
  1.  P = √3 × 415 × I × pf / 1000
        compressor  1.732 × 415 × 135 × 0.90              = 87.33 kW
        condenser   1.732 × 415 ×  32 × 0.88              = 20.24 kW
        chiller pump 1.732 × 415 × 21 × 0.90              = 13.58 kW
        CT fan      1.732 × 415 ×  20 × 0.65              =  9.34 kW
                                                    TOTAL = 130.5 kW

  2.  TR = 23 × 3600 × 1 × 4.5/3024                       = 123.2 TR
  3.  COP = 3,72,600/(87.33 × 0.92 × 860)                 = 5.4
  4.  kW/TR = 130.5/123.2                                 = 1.059
  5.  hydraulic kW = ρgQH/1000:
        chiller pump   23 × 35 × 9.81/1000 = 7.89 → 7.89/13.58   = 58.0 %
        condenser pump 41 × 30 × 9.81/1000 = 12.06 → 12.06/20.24 = 59.6 %

  After: compressor 87.33 × 0.9 = 78.59; pumps at 60 % combined
         7.89/0.6 = 13.15 and 12.06/0.6 = 20.10; CT fan unchanged 9.34
  6.  total 121.18 kW → 121.18/123.2                      = 0.984 kW/TR
  7.  saving = (130.5 − 121.18) × 4500 = 41 940 kWh → × 9 = ₹3,77,460/yr
```

**Note the two different denominators in one question.** COP uses **shaft** power
(87.33 × 0.92) and only the compressor. kW/TR uses **total plant electrical input**
including pumps and the CT fan. That is why 5.4 and 1.059 are not reciprocals of
each other — `3.517/1.059 = 3.32`, not 5.4. **Both are correct; they measure
different boundaries.** Saying so is worth a mark.

*(BEE's key offers the saving both ways — via the kW/TR difference × TR, or via the
kW difference directly. They agree to 1%.)*
</details>

<details><summary>N-3 — boiler and air preheater</summary>

```
  A i)   4.67/100 = m × 0.23 × (145 − 36)/3585  ⟹  m = 6.68 kg dfg/kg coal
         M = 6.68 × 43 000                             = 2,87,240 kg/h
         loss = 2,87,240 × 0.23 × 109                  = 72,01,107 kCal/h
         (or directly: 4.67 % × 3585 = 167.41 kCal/kg × 43 000 = 71,98,630)

  A ii)  3,24,000 × 0.23 × (165 − 37)                  = 95,38,560 kCal/h
  A iii) increase 23,37,453 kCal/h → /3240 = 721.4 kg/h coal
         at 83 % boiler efficiency → 721.4/0.83        = 869 kg/h = 0.869 T/h
  A iv)  0.869 × 7000 × 9500                           = ₹577.97 lakh/yr
  A v)   %O₂ = 21 × EA/(1 + EA):  25 % → 4.2 %,  44.8 % → 6.5 %
         APH leakage = (6.5 − 4.2)/(21 − 6.5) × 100    = 15.86 %

  B i)   APH effectiveness = (294 − 40)/(315 − 40) × 100 = 92.4 %
  B ii)  actual air = A_th × (1 + EA) from the measured O₂
```

**Both inversions in one question.** Part A(i) runs the dry flue gas loss backwards
to recover `m_dfg` from a stated loss percentage. Part A(v) runs the excess air
formula backwards to recover %O₂ from a stated excess air:

```
     EA = O₂/(21 − O₂)      ⟹     O₂ = 21 × EA/(1 + EA)
```

**And note what A(iii) does not do:** it converts the *heat* increase to coal at
the operating GCV of 3240 — correctly, because both conditions are being charged at
the operating fuel. If the question had asked for a coal *difference* across two
different GCVs, `concept-distinctions` entry 14 would apply. It does not here.
Knowing which case you are in is the skill.
</details>
